seq 001 · INDEPENDENT ENFORCEMENT AND EVIDENCE FOR AGENT PAYMENTS

Authorize first.
Keep the evidence.

For x402 payment requests your integration explicitly routes through it, Counterroll applies a deny-by-default policy check. It evaluates supported requests against your policy, fails closed on anything it cannot safely evaluate, and, when a policy-evaluable APPROVE or DENY is recorded successfully, returns a signed, hash-chained record of it before settlement. Records show what Counterroll decided, not that a payment happened. Supported today: the x402 exact scheme with single-key wallets; examples on this page use Base identifiers from a shared demonstration sandbox.

Deny by default Per-agent per-transaction and rolling-window caps Payee, network, and asset controls Decision records you can verify
quickstart.ts — runs against the shared public sandbox, no signup
import { Counterroll, usdc } from '@counterroll/sdk';

// Public sandbox: one published key, bundled demo agents, a live URL.
const cr = new Counterroll({ apiKey: 'qm_sandbox_publicdemo_v1' });

const ok = await cr.check({ agent: 'research-bot', to: 'openai', amount: usdc(1) });
const no = await cr.check({ agent: 'research-bot', to: 'openai', amount: usdc(500) });

console.log(ok.approved, no.reasons); // true  ['per_tx_cap_exceeded']
console.log(no.receipt!.hash);        // signed + hash-chained — verify it yourself

seq 002 · WHAT YOUR TEAM GAINS

Policy checks for routed agent payment requests

A wallet limit can cap an amount. For supported requests that reach policy evaluation, Counterroll records which agent asked, which policy was applied, why the decision was made, and whether the record still verifies later.

01 / CONTROL

Make policy executable

Express per-agent caps for each payment and over a rolling window, plus payee, network, and asset rules. No matching policy or an ambiguous rule means DENY. Malformed inputs are rejected; some rejections are errors rather than signed policy DENYs.

02 / EVIDENCE

Keep the reason, not just the result

Each successfully recorded policy-evaluable APPROVE and DENY is signed and preserves the applied policy snapshot—or its absence—plus decision reasons and a hash-chain link. Some requests refused before policy evaluation are recorded separately without a signature, and some refusals leave no record. A missing record is not proof that no request or payment occurred.

03 / INDEPENDENCE

Keep decision records separate from settlement

Policy and decision history live in Counterroll, not in the facilitator. The upstream x402 facilitator is configured per organization; changing it does not rewrite existing records, and each facilitator still needs to be tested for compatibility.

seq 003 · HOW IT WORKS

check → receipt → chain → verify

1 · check()
Your agent asks first. One call carries the payee, amount, network, and asset of the x402 payment it wants to make. Deny by default: no matching policy or an ambiguous rule comes back DENY. Malformed inputs are rejected; some rejections are errors rather than signed policy DENYs. Nothing approves on an error path.
2 · signed receipt
When a policy-evaluable APPROVE or DENY is recorded successfully, Counterroll returns an attestation signed with your organization's Counterroll Ed25519 key, carrying the exact policy snapshot it was evaluated against. If the record cannot be written, the request fails rather than being approved without one. Requests refused before policy evaluation, such as from unknown agents, with malformed payloads, or with replayed nonces, fail closed and are kept off the chain: some are recorded separately without a signature, and some leave no record. Refusals at settlement time do not produce a signed decision record. A missing record is not proof that no request or payment occurred.
3 · hash chain
Each receipt stores the SHA-256 of the one before it. Ordinary writes are append-only — enforced in code, in review, and by a Postgres trigger that raises on UPDATE or DELETE. A database owner can still bypass triggers, which is why verification checks signatures instead of trusting the database.
4 · verify yourself
cr.verify({ publicKey }) pulls the raw rows and re-derives the chain you were given: each hash recomputed, each signature checked against a public key you authenticated out-of-band. To notice missing or withheld records you also need a trusted reference for the latest expected record. Verification shows the integrity of the records you received; it does not show the chain is complete or current, or that any payment happened.

seq 004 · EVALUATION

Start with an offline evaluation.

The SDK is public — start there. Beyond it, we are open to discussing an offline evaluation that uses synthetic inputs and involves no payments. Production use and embedded platform integrations are exploratory conversations only: they are gated on readiness work and are not an offering available today.

01 · OFFLINE EVALUATION

Test one policy against synthetic requests.

For teams with a named technical owner and a policy they want to test. Inputs are synthetic and no payment is made. What it shows: how the policy decides your test cases, and how the resulting records verify with your own tooling.

02 · PRODUCTION OR EMBEDDED · EXPLORATORY

Talk about later stages, without commitments.

For wallets, facilitators, agent runtimes, marketplaces, and platforms considering production or embedded use. These are exploratory conversations: not available today, gated on readiness work, and not included in an offline evaluation.

STRONG FIT

  • Your agent will initiate x402 payments or paid tool calls.
  • Security or finance needs enforceable limits and decision evidence.
  • You want policy independent of one wallet or facilitator.
  • You have a technical owner who can define test cases.

NOT THE PRODUCT

  • A consumer checkout or human expense card.
  • A wallet, custodian, payroll system, or settlement rail.
  • A promise that every autonomous payment should be approved.
  • A dashboard replacing cryptographic verification.

Bring one payment flow you want to reason about.

In an offline evaluation, synthetic versions of that flow test three questions about whether Counterroll fits your stack.

1 · EnforcementCan the policy fail closed on the exact agent, payee, amount, network, and asset?
2 · EvidenceCan your team re-derive the decision records and verify signatures with a key it authenticated independently?
3 · ArchitectureWould the control remain useful if you changed wallets, facilitators, or settlement infrastructure?

Why not rely only on wallet spending limits?

Wallet limits are useful and Counterroll does not replace them. Counterroll adds a decision layer for each organization: agent identity, a policy assigned to each agent with per-agent per-payment and rolling-window caps, payee and rail restrictions, explicit deny reasons, and a signed policy snapshot for each successfully recorded policy-evaluable decision, kept in a chain you can verify.

Does Counterroll hold funds or settle payments?

Counterroll holds no funds and no settlement keys. It authorizes, attests, and records. When Counterroll approves a settlement request, it proxies that request to the facilitator your organization configures, and the facilitator may then make the payment. Settlement itself happens upstream.

What happens if the Counterroll database is rewritten?

The SDK can pull raw rows and locally re-derive their hashes and signatures against an organization public key you authenticated outside the database. A rewritten chain signed with a different key fails that client-side verification. This detects changes to signed fields; it does not detect records withheld from you unless you also hold a trusted reference for the latest expected record.

What does a DENY look like to the agent?

A DENY is a successful policy evaluation, not an exception. The agent receives approved: false plus machine-readable reasons such as per_tx_cap_exceeded, and a successfully recorded DENY is kept as a signed record. It records Counterroll's refusal; on its own it does not show what happened on the payment rail.

seq 005 · WHY IT EXISTS

Built for the questions that follow an incident.

We review Counterroll internally the way an attacker would. Two findings from internal reviews in July 2026 shaped the architecture. These were internal reviews, not independent audits, and both fixes are recorded in our private repository history.

Finding 001 — the double-spend race

internal review 2026-07-05 · fixed in commit 3a03a17

The window cap said $200 a day. The first build read the agent's window spend, evaluated the policy, then appended the attestation — three steps, no lock. Two near-cap requests arriving at once would each read the same "spent so far," each pass evaluation, and both get approved: window overspent, with two individually valid receipts to show for it. A textbook time-of-check/time-of-use race, in the one component whose job is making spend limits mean something.

The fix put the replay check, the window read, the evaluation, and the append inside a single transaction holding the org's row lock — and the regression test now races concurrent near-cap requests to check it. Nothing evaluates outside that transaction. This fix covers policy decisions; handling of concurrent or retried settlement attempts is a separate problem that is still being worked on.

Finding 002 — the wallet that impersonated an agent

internal review 2026-07-08 · fixed in commit 2b76acc

For drop-in x402 compatibility, the gateway once accepted a fallback: if the agent-signature headers were missing, resolve the agent from the payment's EIP-3009 from wallet. Read that from an attacker's chair. The API key authenticates the tenant, not the agent — so anyone holding the org key could attribute spend to any agent in the org, minting APPROVE attestations and draining that agent's window budget, with no proof of possession of the agent's key. (The org key alone does not supply the payer's EIP-712 signature that settlement needs upstream, but that is not a guarantee against settlement misuse.)

The gateway now requires the agent's Ed25519 request signature by default and rejects the wallet path with 401 before any ledger write; the fallback survives only as an explicit per-org opt-in, documented as the tradeoff it is.

A tamper-evident ledger is only as good as the code that writes to it. Internal reviews continue, and some settlement-time risks remain open. The relevant fixes, tests, and threat-model decisions can be discussed during technical diligence.

seq 006 · INDEPENDENCE

The system that authorizes should not be the only system you must trust.

Counterroll has no custody and holds no settlement keys: v1 authorizes, attests, and records. When it approves a settlement request, it proxies that request to the x402 facilitator your organization configures, and that facilitator may then make the payment. A governance layer is only credible if it can say no to the rail itself — so its records are built to be checked against a key you authenticate yourself, rather than on our word.

NO CUSTODY

No custody, no settlement keys.

Counterroll holds no funds and no settlement keys. Approved settlement requests are proxied to your configured facilitator, which may then make the payment, and your receipts ride in the standard x402 extensions field. Changing the configured facilitator does not rewrite existing records; each facilitator still needs to be tested for compatibility.

NO SILENT FALLBACK

Failure closes, never opens.

Deny by default in the decision path: no matching policy, an ambiguous rule, or a malformed payload all fail closed. An unavailable policy service cannot quietly become permission to pay, and the wallet-impersonation fallback stays off unless an org explicitly opts in.

Verify against the organization's public key, authenticated independently: cr.verify({ publicKey }) re-derives the hashes and signatures of the records you receive against that key — e9d3619e…b6d1 for the public sandbox. A database rewritten and re-signed with a different key fails that check. To notice missing records, compare against a trusted reference for the latest expected record. Verification does not show that the chain is complete or that any payment happened.
